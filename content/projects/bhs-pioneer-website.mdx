## Overview

The public website for the **Pioneer District of the Barbershop Harmony Society** — a real organization with chapters, choruses, quartets, conventions, and decades of history to surface. I designed and built the whole thing solo (my name's in the footer), it's been live in production since 2020, and I'm now re-platforming it from Gatsby to Next.js 15.

## The brief

A volunteer-run music organization needed a site non-technical board members could keep current — events, news, convention info, award and history archives — without touching code, while also handling real donations and growing its mailing list.

## v1 — Gatsby + Sanity (2020–present, live)

The deployed site is a static-generated **Gatsby** front end pulling structured content from **Sanity** via GraphQL. Editors manage news, events, and pages in Sanity's Studio while the site stays fast and secure. It wires in **Stripe** for "Give to Pioneer" donations, **Mailchimp** for email signups, and YouTube for live convention streams, over a deep information architecture (Leadership, Find a Chorus, News & Events, Education, and an extensive History/Hall of Fame section).

## v2 — Next.js 15 re-platform (in progress)

A ground-up rebuild on a modern stack, kept on the `v2-pio-website` branch, structured as a **pnpm monorepo**:

- `apps/web` — **Next.js 15** App Router (Server Components), **TypeScript strict**, **Tailwind v4**, ISR, forms with **React Hook Form + Zod**.
- `apps/studio` — **Sanity Studio v3** (same project `hgayog7e`), **15 content types** — Events (with an approval workflow), News, Choruses (with geolocation), Hall of Fame, District/International Champs, Scoresheets, Board Members, dynamic NavBar, and more.
- Integrations upgraded: **Stripe**, **Resend** for email, **Google Maps** for the chorus directory.
- **CI/CD** to **Vercel** (web) and Sanity Studio, with preview deploys per branch.
- Documented like a real project: a migration plan, architecture decision records, and a schema reference.

## What it demonstrates

Real, shipped, and *maintained* client work: solo design + build, a headless-CMS architecture that hands content control to non-technical users, payment and email integrations — and now a documented production migration from a legacy Gatsby stack to Next.js 15. The v1→v2 arc is a strong "then/now" case study: same product, evolved architecture, real users the whole way.
